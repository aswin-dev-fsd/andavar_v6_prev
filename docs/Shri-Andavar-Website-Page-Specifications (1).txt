HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
This is the content for every page — headings, body copy, button labels, card
and list contents, in the order they appear. Visual design (colours, type,
motion, layout, components) is not specified here; the site will be styled
using a separate reference design system instead.

The site is bilingual (English + Tamil). Tamil copy is included below wherever
it already exists in this spec. Remaining body copy still needs a Tamil-
speaking medical copywriter — do not machine-translate medical content.

Copy marked "EXACT" is final and must be used verbatim.

PART 1 is the shared shell — header, sticky call bar, footer. Identical on all
nine pages.

PARTS 2–10 are the nine pages, each with:
   A · THE BRIEF     what the page is for and who is reading it
   B · CONTENT        every section in order, with the exact copy
   Some pages also carry a short PRODUCTION NOTE where something non-visual
   (consent, verification, a page still to be built) needs flagging.

A · THE BRIEF
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The long-tail search engine of the site: written once, ranks for years. It is
also where fear is dismantled. The structure is fixed — what it is, what happens
on the day, the choices you will be asked to make, the myths, the call — and the

B · CONTENT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  SECTION 1 — PAGE HERO
    Breadcrumb: Home / Treatments / Cataract surgery
    Heading: "Cataract surgery"
    Subheading — EXACT: "In by morning, home by evening, seeing better. The
    most common operation we perform, and the one people worry about most
    unnecessarily."

  SECTION 2 — WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS
    Heading: "What a cataract actually is"
    Paragraph — EXACT: "Behind your pupil sits a clear lens, about the size
    of a lentil, that focuses light onto the back of the eye. With age it
    slowly turns cloudy. Light scatters instead of focusing, and the world
    looks faded, hazy, or as though you are looking through a dirty window."
    Paragraph — EXACT: "It is not a growth, it is not a film over the eye,
    and no drop will clear it. The only treatment that works is to replace
    the lens — which is a fifteen-minute, day-care procedure."
    Diagram (eye cross-section), labelled: Cornea · Clouded lens · Retina
    ("where the image forms")

  SECTION 3 — THE DAY ITSELF
    Eyebrow: "The day itself"
    Heading: "What happens, hour by hour."
    Six steps:
      01 You arrive in the morning — "Fasting is usually not required — you
         will be told beforehand if anything is different in your case.
         Bring someone with you; you will not be able to drive home."
      02 Drops and preparation — "Drops widen the pupil and numb the eye.
         There is no injection and no general anaesthetic for a routine
         cataract."
      03 The operation — about fifteen minutes — "A very small opening is
         made, the cloudy lens is broken up and removed, and a clear
         artificial lens is placed where it was. You are awake, you feel no
         pain, and there is usually no stitch."
      04 Rest, then home — "You rest for a short while and go home the same
         day with a shield over the eye and a schedule of drops."
      05 The day after — "You come back the next morning so Dr. Raghuram can
         check the eye. Most people notice the difference by then."
      06 The weeks after — "Drops continue for a few weeks. Avoid dust,
         swimming and rubbing the eye. A final prescription for glasses is
         given once the eye has settled, usually around four weeks."

  SECTION 4 — CHOICES AND MYTHS
    Card 1 — eyebrow "Lens options":
      Intro: "The artificial lens is a permanent part of the eye, so the
      choice is worth a proper conversation. In broad terms:"
        Monofocal — "Sharp at one distance, usually far. You will still
          need reading glasses. Covered by most schemes."
        Toric — "Corrects astigmatism at the same time, for eyes that have
          it."
        Multifocal — "Aims to reduce dependence on glasses at more than one
          distance. Suits some eyes and some lifestyles, not all."
      Closing line: "Which one suits you depends on your eye and on the
      life you actually lead. Dr. Raghuram will go through it with you
      before anything is decided."
    Card 2 — eyebrow "Worth knowing", three myths:
      "Should I wait until it is ripe?" — "That advice is out of date. A
      cataract does not need to be mature before it is removed, and waiting
      too long makes surgery harder. The right time is when it starts to
      interfere with what you want to do."
      "Will it come back?" — "The cataract itself does not. Some people
      develop a cloudy film behind the new lens months or years later —
      that is cleared in a few minutes with a laser, in the clinic, no
      cutting."
      "Is it covered?" — "Cataract surgery is covered under the schemes we
      accept, subject to their terms. Ask us before you decide — see
      schemes and insurance." (link: "schemes and insurance" → schemes.html)

  SECTION 5 — CLOSING CALL
    Heading — EXACT: "Have it looked at before it starts deciding things

    Subheading — EXACT: "Giving up night driving, reading, or the newspaper
